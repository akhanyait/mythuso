## 1. Who we are

**The rule.** Every surface says what MyThuso is, what GilbertOne is, and that neither is a live service yet — in the same words, because a product that describes itself three different ways has not decided what it is.

### MyThuso

Nurse-led home healthcare for South Africa. A patient books a visit; a SANC-registered nurse comes to the house; a registered doctor reviews what the nurse found. Built by Akhanya IT Innovations, Johannesburg.

### GilbertOne

GilbertOne is a short list of approved answers with a name. It matches what you type, or say on a phone, against that list and replies in sentences people at MyThuso wrote down in advance. GilbertOne is not a doctor, not a nurse and not a person.

`GilbertOne` is the product's name; `Your Thuso AI Doctor` is its descriptor, and the descriptor is never read without its disclosure: `Your Thuso AI Doctor · not a person, and not a doctor`. Powered by ThusoIQ: ThusoIQ is the name of MyThuso's clinical rules platform. In this build GilbertOne uses two things from it and nothing else: the approved sentences in this file and the emergency red flags Thuso SOS already asks about.

### The honesty line every surface carries

The public page's notice bar, word for word from `apps/web/src/features/Landing.tsx`: **MyThuso is in development. This page describes a service being built in Johannesburg. Nothing here books a visit, takes a payment or sends a nurse anywhere.**

Inside the product the shell's badge reads `Design preview · Fictional data` (`packages/catalog/locales.json`, `shell.previewBadge`), and every screen that depends on something not yet connected renders that capability's own notice from `packages/catalog/capabilities.json` — for the connected kit: `No device is connected. These readings are sample data and no instrument has been paired.`

- **one-notice-per-screen.** A screen renders the notice for the capability it depends on. Not three of them stacked, and not one it does not use.
- **the-notice-is-the-contract.** No screen writes its own not-connected sentence. It names a capability and the sentence is rendered from this file.
- **connected-needs-evidence.** A capability may be marked connected only when `evidence` names a file that exists and `blockedBy` is empty. The build refuses it otherwise.

From CLAUDE.md, the rule under all of these: "Nothing here is a real service. No visit is booked, no payment taken, no clinical decision issued, no device contacted. Every screen that could be mistaken for the real thing says so."

### Every foreground on every surface, measured

WCAG 2.2 AA. 4.5:1 for body text, 3:1 for large text (18.66px bold or 24px and over) and for the visual boundary of a control or a focus indicator (SC 1.4.11). The focus indicator is two rings, not one: no single colour clears 3:1 against both a white card and a forest panel, and controls exist on both. A light inner ring carries the dark grounds and a dark outer ring carries the light ones, so whichever ground it lands on, one of the two is legible. Tying the ring to --amber, which also labels warnings, made the two pull against each other the moment the warning was darkened enough to be readable.

### The scale, by role

| Step | Pixels | Where it is read |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `screenTitle` | 28 | The title of a screen |
| `sectionTitle` | 18 | A section heading |
| `heading` | 22 | A card that leads a screen |
| `cardTitle` | 16 | A card title |
| `body` | 15 | Body text |
| `caption` | 13 | Captions, helper text, metadata — the smallest size rendered |
| `metric` | 32 | A figure in a row of figures |
| `metricLarge` | 40 | The one figure a screen leads with (the web takes it above 900 px) |

Minimum body size 14 px; nothing renders below 13 px, and `tests/accessibility.spec.ts` measures rendered text against that floor. The floor tests/accessibility.spec.ts enforces on rendered text. It used to be 11 while the same file called 13 the minimum for body text — a two-point gap that unread counts, chart axes and tab-bar labels lived in. The gap is closed rather than described: the caption size is now 13 and the floor is the caption size, so the two agree. That is a type-scale decision with a layout consequence on every dense screen, and the layouts were changed to take it rather than the number being nudged to fit them.

The handoff states no type sizes, so the scale below is unchanged. Six steps became seven. Both native passes stopped at the same gap: sectionTitle is 18 and screenTitle is 28, and a card that leads a screen wants something between them — every one of them had reached for 22 and typed it. A step nobody can find is a step everybody invents.

Two steps, because a metric is the design's signature and it is read at two distances. `metric` (32) is a figure in a row of them; `metricLarge` (40) is the one figure a screen leads with, and the web takes it above 900px. They were 32 on iOS and 40-then-42 on the web with only 32 declared, which meant the shape the whole language is built around was a different size on two platforms and nothing said which was right.

> Use `font-display` for page titles and compact feature headings; use `font-sans` everywhere else. Headlines are confident and concise. Supporting language is plain, specific, and human. Prefer “Review patient record” to “Unlock intelligent insights.” Labels describe actions directly. Avoid jargon, hype, and exclamation-heavy writing.
> — the handoff's design-guidelines.md, "Typography and voice"

### The signal dot

The lime signal point every icon carries, top right. It is the one thing in the family that may move, and only when the action it marks is live or time-sensitive: a visit that is happening, a result that has just arrived, a message waiting. Scale only, so the icon's box never changes under a thumb. A circle at (18.6, 5.4) with radius 2.05, filled highlight and stroked primary at 1. ## 6. Components

**The rule.** Compose a screen from the shared components by their named variants and sizes; where the build departs from the handoff, the component's own file says which rule of this build required it.

The library is `apps/web/src/ui`: the handoff's component library rebuilt on plain CSS classes that read only the token variables, with the handoff's names, props, variants and sizes. 19 components are in the handoff's catalogue; 17 files are in the barrel, and GilbertOne and MyThusoHealthIcon are not rebuilt as components because the first is the rig and the logo of chapter 10 and the second is the icon family of chapter 5.

### Alert

- **variant:** `info`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`

**The handoff says:** Use for important contextual feedback that should remain visible. Example: `<Alert variant="success" title="Record updated" />`. **Do not:** Do not use alerts for routine helper text.

**This build:** Feedback that stays on screen. Every variant pairs its Lucide icon and a title in words with its colour, so no alert is told apart by colour alone, and a danger alert is role="alert" — announced the moment it appears — while the rest are role="status" and wait their turn. Two of the handoff's icon colours cannot be seen on a light card: its warning is the brand lime at 1.21:1 and its danger the brand orange at 2.99:1, both under the 3:1 a meaningful graphic needs. On a light ground those two icons are drawn in the replacements tokens.json#contrast.knownFailures names (#92400e and #b42318, already the build's --mango-ink and --danger); on a dark ground in the handoff's own colours, which clear it.

### Avatar

- **size:** `sm`, `md`, `lg`

Exports: `Avatar`, `AvatarImage`, `AvatarFallback`.

**The handoff says:** Use for a person or organization identity. Example: `<Avatar><AvatarFallback initials="ND" /></Avatar>`. **Do not:** Do not use decorative images as avatars.

**This build:** A person's or an organisation's identity. Never a decorative image, and never a button on its own — a control that opens a profile wraps it in one. The fallback's initials are the handoff's primary on a primary tint on a light ground and the handoff's dark accent on a dark one, because its dark primary as text on a tint does not clear 4.5:1.

### Badge

- **variant:** `neutral`, `primary`, `accent`, `success`, `warning`, `danger`
- **size:** `sm`, `md`

**The handoff says:** Use for compact metadata, categories, and non-urgent states. Example: `<Badge variant="success" dot>Verified</Badge>`. **Do not:** Do not use a badge as a button.

**This build:** Compact metadata — a category, a non-urgent state. Never a button: it has no press and no focus. Both sizes set their words at 13, the smallest size this product renders; the handoff's 11 and 12 are below it, so sm and md differ by their padding. The danger badge's words are the measured replacement (#b42318 on light, the handoff's orange on dark) because the brand orange as text measures 2.99:1, and the primary badge's words on dark are the handoff's dark accent, because its dark primary as text on a tint measures under 4.5:1.

### Button

- **variant:** `primary`, `accent`, `secondary`, `ghost`, `destructive`
- **size:** `sm`, `md`, `lg`, `icon`

**The handoff says:** Use for clear actions in clinical workflows. Example: `<Button>Open patient record</Button>`. **Do not:** Do not use multiple primary buttons in one action group.

**This build:** The handoff's Button, with its cva variants and sizes kept by name so a screen written against the handoff's catalogue reads the same here: primary, accent, secondary, ghost and destructive; sm, md, lg and icon. The classes are ui.css's and read nothing but token variables. Three things differ from the handoff, each for a rule of this build rather than for taste: - md and icon are 44 tall rather than 40, and sm keeps its 32-pixel face over a 44-pixel hit area (ui.css, "Targets"). A health app is used one-handed in a doorway. - The loading glyph turns once as it arrives rather than spinning for as long as the wait lasts: a spinner that runs forever is the one piece of motion this codebase refuses outright. The button still says it is busy — aria-busy, and it cannot be pressed twice. - A button is type="button" unless the caller says otherwise, so a Button dropped into a form does not submit it by accident.

### Card

- **variant:** `default`, `elevated`, `interactive`
- **padding:** `none`, `sm`, `md`, `lg`

Exports: `Card`, `CardHeader`, `CardTitle`, `CardDescription`, `CardContent`.

**The handoff says:** Use to frame one coherent object or tool. Example: `<Card padding="md">Patient summary</Card>`. **Do not:** Do not nest cards inside cards.

**This build:** One coherent object or tool, framed. The handoff's three variants are kept: default sits on the card shadow, elevated on the raised one, and interactive answers a pointer with its border — never two shadows at once, since elevation is one shadow in this system. An interactive card is still a <div>: what a person presses inside it is a Button or a link the caller puts there, because a div that pretends to be a button has to reimplement the keyboard and forgets some of it.

### Checkbox

**The handoff says:** Use for independent binary choices or multi-select lists. Example: `<Checkbox label="Patient consent confirmed" />`. **Do not:** Do not use for mutually exclusive options.

**This build:** An independent yes-or-no, drawn over the native checkbox so Space, the form and the announced state are the browser's. The box is the handoff's sixteen pixels; the label around it is the target and is 44 tall, which is the exemption tokens.json already writes down for an input a design hides behind its own label. Checked differs from unchecked by the drawn tick, never by the fill alone.

### Divider

- **orientation:** `horizontal`, `vertical`

**The handoff says:** Use sparingly to separate adjacent content groups. Example: `<Divider />`. **Do not:** Do not use when spacing alone communicates the grouping.

**This build:** A rule between two groups, used sparingly: spacing is usually the better separator. It is an <hr>, so it is a separator to assistive technology, with its orientation said.

### Field

**The handoff says:** Use to connect a label, control, hint, and validation message. Example: `<Field label="Email" htmlFor="email"><Input id="email" /></Field>`. **Do not:** Do not duplicate error text outside the field.

**This build:** A label, its control, and the hint or the error beneath it, tied together by ids. The handoff's Field prints the message under an id and leaves the control to point at it, which means every caller has to remember aria-describedby and some will not. Here Field does the pointing: its one control child is handed aria-describedby (added to any the caller already gave), aria-invalid while there is an error, and aria-required when the field is required. The asterisk is hidden from assistive technology because aria-required already says the same thing in words. An error replaces the hint rather than sitting beside it, as in the handoff — two sentences under one field is one more than a person reads.

### IconButton

- **size:** `sm`, `md`, `lg`

**The handoff says:** Use for familiar compact toolbar actions with an accessible label. Example: `<IconButton label="Search"><Search /></IconButton>`. **Do not:** Never omit the label prop.

**This build:** A button that is only an icon, so its name is required rather than hoped for: `label` becomes the aria-label a screen reader announces and the title a pointer shows. The icon inside is the caller's and is decorative — Lucide marks its own svg aria-hidden. The handoff's three sizes are 32, 40 and 48; here md is 44 and sm keeps a 32-pixel face over a 44-pixel hit area, as Button's sizes do.

### Input

**The handoff says:** Use for single-line clinical and account data. Example: `<Input placeholder="Search patients" />`. **Do not:** Do not use without a visible label in forms.

**This build:** A single line of clinical or account data. `invalid` is aria-invalid, and the stylesheet draws the invalid state from that attribute rather than from a class, so the state a screen reader announces and the state a reader sees cannot disagree. Its visible label is the caller's — Field, or a <label> of their own — because a placeholder is not a label.

### MetricCard

**The handoff says:** Use for one high-value operational metric and optional trend. Example: `<MetricCard label="Facilities" value="1,240" trend="+8% this year" />`. **Do not:** Do not place prose in the value slot.

**This build:** One figure a screen is opened for, with its label and an optional trend. The value is the metric size of the type scale (32), not the handoff's 30, which the scale does not have. The trend is the handoff's success colour only where that colour can carry words: on a light ground the handoff's green measures 3.64:1 on white and clears the 3:1 floor for a mark but not 4.5:1 for text, so there it is drawn in the teal ink the build already measures for "a value in range", and on a dark ground in the handoff's own green, which reads. The trend's words say which way it went; the colour never has to.

### NavigationItem

**The handoff says:** Use for destinations inside a product navigation rail. Example: `<NavigationItem active>Patients</NavigationItem>`. **Do not:** Do not use for submit actions.

**This build:** A destination in a navigation rail. The current one carries aria-current="page", and on screen the handoff's aqua tint, its words in the ink colour, and a heavier weight — so it is never told apart by colour alone. 44 tall rather than the handoff's 40. A count, when there is one, replaces the chevron; the count is text, so it is announced with the destination's name.

### Select

**The handoff says:** Use for a constrained list of familiar options. Example: `<Select><option>Cardiology</option></Select>`. **Do not:** Do not use for only two binary choices.

**This build:** A native <select> with the handoff's chevron drawn over it. The chevron is a sibling that lets the pointer through, so the whole field opens the list, and the native element keeps what a rebuilt one would have to reimplement: the keyboard, type-ahead, the platform's own picker on a phone.

### Spinner

- **size:** `sm`, `md`, `lg`
- **tone:** `primary`, `accent`, `current`

**The handoff says:** Use for indeterminate waits where progress cannot be measured. Example: `<Spinner label="Loading patient record" />`. **Do not:** Do not show for instantly completed actions.

**This build:** An indeterminate wait, announced: role="status" with its label in words for a screen reader. The handoff spins it for as long as the wait lasts. This build refuses a spinner that runs forever — apps/web/src/surface/app.css says so above the one screen that waits for a chunk — because an endless animation is motion nobody asked for and nobody can stop. So the arc turns once as it arrives and then rests, and the ring with its one coloured quarter is the familiar mark of a wait without moving. Under reduced motion it does not turn at all.

### StatusIndicator

- **status:** `online`, `busy`, `offline`

**The handoff says:** Use for live availability and operational state. Example: `<StatusIndicator status="online" label="Available" />`. **Do not:** Do not communicate status with color alone.

**This build:** Live availability, said in words: `label` is required and is the thing a reader reads, and the dot beside it is decoration hidden from assistive technology. ### Endless motion, the pause control and reduced motion

Nothing on a screen runs for ever except what is explicitly ambient and gated: the signal dot on a live icon, the public page's two loops (its particles and the badge's pulse). Every such animation sits behind `[data-decor="on"]` on the document element, so one control — the button labelled `Pause motion` in `apps/web/src/components/MotionPause.tsx` — stops all of it, which is what WCAG 2.2.2 asks for. A spinner that spins for as long as a wait lasts is refused outright: the loading arc turns once as it arrives and rests.

respectReducedMotion is not a suggestion and not a speed setting. Reduced motion REMOVES these animations rather than shortening them: a 420ms entrance run at 80ms is still a thing that moved. Web answers prefers-reduced-motion, iOS answers accessibilityReduceMotion, and Android follows the system animator duration scale — which reports 0 when a reader has turned animations off, so a duration multiplied by it is genuinely none.

Glass: A glass panel may not sit on top of text. Blur behind a word is the one place translucency stops being decoration and starts being a legibility problem. backdrop-filter is expensive on the mid-range Android handsets this product is actually for. Where it is unsupported — and inside @media (prefers-reduced-transparency: reduce) — glass resolves to the opaque floor colour, which is exactly the colour every contrast figure was measured against. The fallback is not a degradation; it is the measured case.

## 9. Voice and words

### Wording a patient may never read

Nothing addressed to a patient tells them they have a condition. A finding is consistent with something, for a clinician to confirm with them. A diagnosis is a registered clinician's, given to a patient in a conversation that can answer the questions it raises. Software that tells somebody what they have has diagnosed them with nobody accountable, and ThusoIQ Master v3.5 §5.3 holds a finding to "consistent with". The phrases are wording, not clinical content: they name no condition, and the build refuses every one of them in any patient-facing sentence. The build refuses these phrases in any patient-facing sentence: "you have been diagnosed", "you are diagnosed", "you've been diagnosed", "your diagnosis is", "you are suffering from", "you're suffering from", "you have a condition", "you have the condition", "you have a disease", "you have an illness", "you have an infection", "you have a disorder", "you have got a", "you've got a".

Nothing is written to browser storage of any kind. The contract's own refusal, `no-audio-kept`: No recording of your voice is made or kept — not on the phone, not anywhere else. The microphone's sound is read once for its loudness and handed to the phone's own recogniser, and nothing writes it to a file. The build refuses the recording and audio-file APIs in both apps. (`assistant.json#voice.audioStored` is false.) One utterance may run 45 seconds before GilbertOne prompts the person to continue, and the web's 30-second listening cap stands in front of it — `assistant.json#voice.listeningDecision`, decided by the Founder on 2026-09-21: Amended: conversation mode with wake-word and push-to-talk fallback, replacing the tap-only gesture of 14 September. The microphone still closes on its own after 30 seconds. Changing the cap or the gesture remains the founder's decision, not an edit.

- There is no wake word on the web. The on-device wake-word engine the amendment names is a native dependency the web build forbids, so nothing listens for a name: the conversation starts with one tap and is hands-free from there.
- It is not always listening. The microphone closes on Stop, when the panel closes, after the idle rounds run out and after an emergency answer, and it never opens without a tap having started the conversation.
- It is not a recording. No audio is captured, buffered or kept, and no cloud speech service hears the person for the on-device conversation.
- It does not decide anything. A reply is whatever GilbertOne already says in text; hearing it and answering hands-free changes how the words arrive and nothing about what they are.

### The emergency face

A16 is the one cue on the safety track: a steady, neutral-attentive face that stops yawns, smiles and decorative motion, surfaces the action immediately, and holds until Start again — the patient's own reset. Nothing displaces it but itself; the ambulance numbers are the first thing in the answer and the face does nothing that competes with them.

Stable, neutral-attentive face. Stop yawns, smiles and decorative motion. Surface the approved action immediately.

In hands-free conversation the microphone closes after an emergency answer: After an emergency answer the next thing that should happen is a phone call, not another turn with GilbertOne. A microphone reopening on its own at that moment invites the person to keep talking to a page instead of ringing for help, and a page hearing an ambulance call in progress is a microphone open for no reason the person chose. The numbers stay on the screen because the answer is what matters, and the reading aloud was only a second way to receive it.

## 11. Accessibility

**The rule.** A control is 44 pixels, a word clears 4.5:1, focus is visible on every ground, everything works from the keyboard, every spoken word is also written, and motion is removed — not shortened — for a reader who asked for stillness.

**Targets.** A control smaller than a fingertip is not a smaller control, it is a control somebody misses while standing in a doorway holding a child. WCAG 2.2 puts the AA floor at 24x24 (SC 2.5.8) and the AAA figure at 44x44 (SC 2.5.5); this design is held to 44 because it is a health app operated one-handed, and everything that is not is listed below with what it measures instead. tests/accessibility.spec.ts reads this list, so an exemption is a line in the contract rather than a line in a test.

| Exempted control | Measured | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `.hero-dots button` | 24 px | Twenty-four square, the AA floor, and every slide it selects is also reachable from the pause control and by letting the carousel run. Making it 44 would space three dots across 132px and turn a quiet indicator into the loudest thing on the banner. |
| `.text-button` | 26 px | An inline text action inside a dense row — a provenance note, a call roster line. At 44 it would double the height of every row it sits in. Every one of them has a full-size equivalent elsewhere on the screen. |
| `.avatar.small` | 36 px | Only undersized where it is not a button. Where it is one — the profile control in the top bar — it is 44. |

A link inside a running paragraph, and an input the design hides behind its own label — the label is the target and is measured in its place.

**Contrast.** A palette is either measured or it is a matter of opinion, and colour is the one part of a design where an opinion held by somebody with ordinary eyesight is worth nothing to the reader who cannot use the result. Every foreground and background this design actually puts together is listed below with the ratio it has to clear, and scripts/check-boundaries.mjs computes each one from the hexes above on every build. A pair that is not listed is a pair nobody has checked, which is why adding a colour combination to the design means adding a row here first. Vital Teal and Mango appear here only against the 3:1 non-text floor: at 2.49:1 and 1.78:1 on white they are brand marks, not labels, and the two ink variants beside them are what carries the words.

**Focus.** On the shared components: The handoff's @layer base rule — a 2-pixel ring-coloured outline 2 pixels out — on every focusable component, including the ones whose handoff classes remove it (focus-visible:outline-none on Button, Tab and the fields, which left a keyboard user nowhere). The ring is the handoff's green: 3.4:1 on white and more on the dark ground, over the 3:1 a focus indicator needs, and it lands on the ground outside the control rather than on the control's own fill. core.css's two-ring focus (mango inside ink) stays on everything that has not adopted these components; the box-shadow it adds is replaced by the component's own shadow, so focus never stacks a second one. .is-focus is the gallery's forced state, so a screenshot can show focus beside rest. On every screen that has not adopted them: Two rings, not one. A control can sit on a white card or on a forest panel, and no single colour clears 3:1 against both — so the light inner ring carries the dark grounds and the dark outer ring carries the light ones. Whichever it lands on, one of the two is legible.

**Keyboard.** Every action uses its native element — a Button is a `<button>`, a Select a `<select>`, a Checkbox an `<input>`, a Divider an `<hr>` — so Space, Enter, the arrows and the platform's own picker are the browser's. A tab list is one Tab stop with the arrow keys, Home and End moving between tabs. A card that looks pressable is still a `<div>`; what a person presses inside it is a Button or a link.

**Captions.** A spoken reply is a second reading of words already on the screen: the caption is the reply itself, written in full before the voice starts and for as long as it speaks, and no setting anywhere turns the written words off (`packages/catalog/assistant.json#voice.webSpeech`). The rig carries `aria-hidden` and no text; what GilbertOne is doing is said in words beside it, because §10 of its scope forbids saying anything by motion or colour alone.

**Motion.** respectReducedMotion is not a suggestion and not a speed setting. Reduced motion REMOVES these animations rather than shortening them: a 420ms entrance run at 80ms is still a thing that moved. Web answers prefers-reduced-motion, iOS answers accessibilityReduceMotion, and Android follows the system animator duration scale — which reports 0 when a reader has turned animations off, so a duration multiplied by it is genuinely none.

**What is measured on the web**, by `tests/accessibility.spec.ts` on both viewports: no horizontal overflow at a 320 px viewport, which is narrower than any phone still sold and is no horizontal overflow with the layout viewport halved, which is what 200 % browser zoom does to a no interactive control smaller than 44×44 CSS pixels; no rendered text below the minimum body size in `packages/design-tokens/tokens.json`.

**What is not yet done.** Screen-reader testing is still not done anywhere, and neither is testing on real hardware.

## 12. Platforms

**The rule.** One token reaches three platforms through a generator, never through a copy; and a phone does not yet do everything the web does, which this chapter says plainly.

`scripts/emit-tokens.mjs` writes `packages/design-tokens/tokens.json` into `apps/web/src/tokens.generated.css` (`--<colour>`, `--color-<role>`, `--r-<radius>`, `--space-<n>`, `--shadow` / `--shadow-raised`, `--font-display` / `--font-text`, `--t-quick` / `--t-settle` / `--t-enter`, `--ease-soft`), into `apps/ios/MyThuso/DesignSystem/Tokens.swift` (`ThusoTheme`, `ThusoSemantic.Light` / `.Dark`, `ThusoRadius`) and into `apps/android/app/src/main/java/za/co/mythuso/ui/Tokens.kt` (the same names as Kotlin objects). The dark roles reach the web only under `:root[data-theme="dark"]`: light is the default whatever the reader's system says, and dark is a switch a person turns on (the founder's decision of 29 September 2026). `scripts/emit-icons.mjs` writes the icon family the same way: `apps/web/src/ui/icons/MyThusoIcons.generated.tsx`, one vector drawable per icon under `apps/android/app/src/main/res/drawable`, and `apps/ios/MyThuso/Models/MyThusoIconsData.swift`. The build compares every generated file with what its generator returns, so a stale file and a hand-edited one fail the same way.
