## Out of scope for now, and why

- **Ultrasound (#19, #21).** Scanning is a clinical scope and training decision before it is a purchase. No nurse
  here scans, and no one would read the images.
- **The hearing aid (#20).** No South African over-the-counter route was found. In 2013 the HPCSA warned that
  selling hearing aids without its registration breaks the law.
- **Palm-vein biometrics (#25).** A palm-vein template is special personal information under POPIA, and MyThuso
  takes no biometrics.
- **The tamper-proof retention band (#15).** It is a means of keeping a person somewhere.
- **The sleep-apnoea kit (#12).** It is a doctor's diagnostic study, and it uploads to the maker's cloud.
- **Gateways and hubs (#16, #17).** They forward every reading to a supplier's platform.
- **Ward and building equipment (#2, #3, #29).** Bedside tablets and wall terminals need a ward or a building, and
  MyThuso has neither.
- **The dispensary handheld (#28).** It is built to run a dispensary, and MyThuso dispenses nothing.
- **The alarm console (#5).** Its minimum order is 1,000.
- **The medical watch (#1).** It is a vital-signs watch with medical claims.

## Supplements

The shop sells no supplement or vitamin, and the build fails if one is listed. The twenty are recorded so that you
can decide. Before any decision:

- **Twelve make a claim that would be unlawful to repeat** to a patient: #31, #32, #33, #36, #37, #38, #39, #41,
  #44, #47, #49 and #50. These are claims to treat or prevent a disease or to modify a bodily function, which an
  unregistered complementary medicine may not make.
- **#32 is a skin-lightening claim.** Its "inhibits tyrosinase … even skin tone" carries a history no health service
  should repeat. Oral glutathione's only permitted claim is as an antioxidant.
- **Three carry interaction risks:**
  - #41: berberine lowers blood glucose. It comes from a one-year trading firm offering dropshipping.
  - #39: vitamin K changes how warfarin works.
  - #47: ginkgo thins the blood.
- **Several certification claims mean less than they appear to.** "FDA registered" (#32, #42) is not an approval.
  "NSF-compliant" (#38) is not "certified". CE and ISO 13485 on a vitamin (#50) are medical-device marks.
- **Every one would need its own paperwork.** That means the SAHPRA Category D label words, low-risk claims only,
  and a certificate of analysis per lot from a laboratory that is not the supplier's.

## What must be in hand before any order

The register lists these on the Control Tower page.

1. **A physical sample**, tested by whoever would use it.
2. **A binding specification sheet**: model, firmware, radio bands and accessories.
3. **The certificates themselves**, checked against each issuer's public register. A picture on a listing is not
   enough.
4. **For anything taken by mouth**, an independent certificate of analysis per lot.
5. **The data flow in writing**: where every reading goes, who holds it, in which country, and whether it can come
   into Thuso instead.
6. **The supplier's business licence and registration number.**
7. **A named South African importer.** It must hold the **SAHPRA medical device establishment licence** for class B
   and above, and **ICASA type approval** for every radio. That includes Bluetooth, Wi-Fi, LoRa and NFC. A LoRa
   device must be set to a South African band.
8. **RICA-registered SIMs** for anything with its own SIM.
9. **An NRCS scope ruling** on each charger.
10. **For ECGs, blood pressure and physiological monitors, and ultrasound**: a question to SAHPRA Radiation Control,
    because these are listed electronic products under the Hazardous Substances Act.
11. **Incoterms and Trade Assurance** written into the order.

MyThuso holds none of these today. It has no establishment licence and no ICASA approvals.
